How we test the candle fade

This is the strategy that is live, written as the test we use to see whether it works. Seven markets, 39 slices. A large up minute is faded short. A large down minute is faded long. The order is a limit. The stop is on the order. Profit is added when the trade closes.

The test account starts at $20,000. That is not the live wallet. Window: 1 Jan 2022 through 26 Oct 2026, 07:33 UTC.

Each year, started again from $20,000

A fresh year puts the cash back to $20,000 on 1 January and walks that year alone. A year cannot look rich because a previous year compounded it. The order of the test

  1. The bar has to close. The signal is that minute's open-to-close body, as a fraction of the close, and its volume divided by the average volume of the previous ten minutes. The signal minute is not inside that average.
  2. The body and the volume have to sit in a slice. The 39 slices are listed below. No volume figure means that slice does not use a volume test. An up bar fades short. A down bar fades long.
  3. The entry is a limit, past the close, in bodies of that candle. On Bitcoin, Ether, BNB, Solana, and XRP the limit is rested 0.50 bodies back toward the close, and never inside 0.05 bodies of the close. If that rest would reach the target, it is not used. The stop and the target stay measured from the unrested price. Six slices use a nearer target when the fade is short. That column is "Short target".
  4. The fill is a later bar. If price does not trade the limit before the entry hold runs out, there is no trade and no chase. The fill bar can stop the trade. It cannot pay the target. A gap through the stop is booked at that bar's open. If a later bar touches the stop and the target, the stop is the exit.
  5. Then the position clock. Where the table says a number, that is how long the filled trade may run, in minutes. "Entry hold" means the filled trade uses the same clock as the entry. "Off" means the trade waits for the target or the stop. A clock exit is marked at that bar's close.
  6. One open trade per coin. A new signal on a coin that is already in a trade is skipped.
  7. Size, then the liquidation cut. Notional starts from the wallet, the stop distance, the body, and a weight: Bitcoin 2, Ether 1, BNB 0.5, Solana 0.5, XRP 0.5, Sui 1, gold 1.25. Bitcoin is capped at 1,500 coins. Leverage is 20. Cross margin, so the whole balance is the margin. Maintenance is notional times the tier rate, minus the deduction. An empty deduction is 0. If maintenance of the coins already open plus this order is at least the balance, the order does not open. Otherwise size is cut until the liquidation price sits past the stop, and until a stop on every open coin would still leave the wallet above maintenance. If it cannot be cut that far, or the cut is under $5, the order is not sent. A wick through the stop is the stop. If the path's worst move reaches the liquidation distance first, the loss is booked as a liquidation.
  8. Fees and when profit counts. A target pays 4 bps. A stop, a clock exit, or a gap pays 7.5 bps. The result is added to the balance when the trade closes, not when it opens.

The live process also moves 3% of a green week's Unified gain into Funding at Monday 00:00 SAST. This walk does not. Every close stays in the futures balance. A research cancel that pulls an unfilled entry when the body is reclaimed is not in the live book and is not in this walk.

What this page is not

It is not a seat, a copy-trading link, or a fund. It is the worksheet for one book: does the rule still pay when the fill cannot use the signal bar, the target cannot pay on the fill bar, the stop wins a tie, liquidation is on, and the account is restarted each year?

Leveraged perpetual trading can wipe an account. The path is a historical backtest on Bybit one-minute data through 26 Oct 2026, 07:33 UTC. The maintenance ladder is today's Bybit ladder, applied across the whole window. The full-window result is in-sample. Nothing here is a promise of future results, and nothing here is the live wallet.
